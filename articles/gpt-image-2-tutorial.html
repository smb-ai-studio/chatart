GPT IMAGE 2 TUTORIAL

How to use GPT Image 2 for product visuals, posters, and ad variants.

GPT Image 2 becomes more useful when you stop asking for “a nice image” and start defining an asset system. This tutorial shows how to move from a product brief to a hero visual, text-aware poster, thumbnail, and controlled edit while protecting product details and campaign claims.

Summary for fast readers

  • Define the asset job before describing the style: hero image, poster, thumbnail, ad variant, or edit.
  • Use reference images to anchor product identity, composition, or visual tone, and state which details must not change.
  • Keep in-image copy short, exact, and separated from claims that still need human review.
  • Generate one controlled direction first, then create variants by changing one variable at a time.

WHAT THE SOURCES CONFIRM

GPT Image 2 is most useful when the image has a job.

OpenAI's current prompting guidance describes image generation as an image-in, image-out workflow that can use reference inputs, structured instructions, and edits. Its public materials emphasize following detailed visual instructions, working with text and layout, and refining an image rather than treating the first render as the finished design.

That does not mean every output is automatically production-ready. Product labels, small type, prices, claims, faces, and fine details still need inspection. The best workflow makes those review points explicit before the image is exported or placed in an ad.

THE WORKFLOW

Follow eight steps from product brief to approved variant.

  1. Define the asset. Say whether you need a product hero, poster, thumbnail, ad image, editorial scene, or edit. Include where it will appear.
  2. Write the product truth. List the shape, material, color, label, camera-facing side, and any detail that must stay recognizable. Separate confirmed facts from creative direction.
  3. Choose the composition. Describe subject placement, camera angle, negative space, crop, and what the viewer should notice first.
  4. Set the visual treatment. Add lighting, surface, environment, palette, lens feel, and the emotional tone that supports the campaign.
  5. Add text only when it has a purpose. Provide the exact headline, hierarchy, placement, and safe margin. Keep the copy short enough to inspect.
  6. Generate one proof. Check product fidelity, composition, text, and unintended objects before making more variants.
  7. Edit one variable at a time. Ask to change the background, crop, color mood, or headline space while preserving the product and camera angle.
  8. Build the variant matrix. Expand the approved route into platform crops, thumbnails, ad angles, and supporting visuals, then run the same QA checklist again.

PROMPT FRAMEWORK

Write prompts in layers: job, subject, composition, treatment, constraints.

Product hero prompt

Create a landscape campaign hero image for [product] used on [landing page or ad placement]. Subject: [product description and non-negotiable details]. Composition: product on the [left/right/center], [amount] of clean negative space for headline, camera at [angle], crop-safe edges. Scene: [environment]. Lighting: [lighting]. Material detail: [texture/reflection]. Mood: [tone]. Do not change the product shape, label layout, or color. No extra logos, no invented claims, no unreadable text.

Poster with exact copy

Create a vertical promotional poster for [event/product]. Use the uploaded product image as the identity reference. Place the exact headline “[HEADLINE]” at the top, the short support line “[SUPPORT LINE]” below it, and “[CTA]” in a button-like area near the bottom. Keep all text inside safe margins, use a clear hierarchy, and leave the product unobstructed. Do not add words or change the provided copy.

Controlled edit

Edit the uploaded image by changing only the background from [old setting] to [new setting]. Keep the product, label, camera angle, lighting direction, scale, and shadow shape unchanged. Match the new background's perspective and color temperature to the original. Do not add text or extra objects.

TEXT, EDITS, AND QUALITY

Use the model for exploration, then review like a designer.

For text-heavy layouts, provide short exact copy and a clear hierarchy. Ask for one headline, one support line, or one CTA before trying a paragraph. Inspect the image at the size and crop where people will actually see it; a poster that looks readable at full resolution may fail as a mobile thumbnail.

For edits, use the “change only X; preserve Y” pattern. Name the invariant details: product silhouette, person identity, logo placement, camera angle, shadow, or color temperature. If several things are wrong, repair them in separate passes so you can tell which instruction caused the change.

DELIVERY CHECKLIST

Approve the image only after these checks pass.

Product fidelity

Shape, label, material, color, and key details match the approved reference.

Composition

The focal point is clear, the crop is safe, and headline or CTA space is intentional.

Text

Copy is exact, legible, correctly spelled, and still readable at the final placement size.

Claims

Nothing in the visual implies a performance, price, testimonial, or result that the brief cannot support.

Variants

Each version changes a planned variable rather than drifting into a different campaign.

Rights and access

Check source-image permissions, likeness, logo usage, current tool terms, and commercial approval before delivery.

COMMON MISTAKES

Keep the image workflow controlled.

Style before purpose

A glossy style cannot rescue a visual with no clear placement, audience, or message.

Too much copy

Long paragraphs make in-image typography harder to inspect. Use the image for a short message and place detail in HTML or the caption.

Changing everything at once

Hold the product, crop, and message steady while testing one new background or lighting route.

Skipping final-size review

Check the asset where it will appear: mobile feed, thumbnail, poster preview, or landing-page hero.

FAQ

GPT Image 2 questions for practical image production.

Is GPT Image 2 only for making brand-new images?

No. OpenAI's public guidance describes image inputs and editing workflows as well as generation. Use a reference image when you need to preserve product identity or a visual direction, and state what must remain unchanged.

How much detail should a prompt include?

Include the details a reviewer can observe: asset job, subject, composition, lighting, text, crop, and constraints. Add more detail when it removes a real ambiguity; avoid decorative filler.

Can I trust text inside the generated image?

Treat it as a draft until you inspect the exact wording and final-size readability. Keep copy short, provide it verbatim, and verify every claim and brand term manually.

Should I use GPT Image 2 for final advertising artwork?

Use it to explore and refine campaign directions, then apply human review for product accuracy, typography, claims, rights, accessibility, and channel requirements before publishing.
